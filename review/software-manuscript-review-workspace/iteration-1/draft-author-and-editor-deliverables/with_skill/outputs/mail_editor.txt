Recommendation: Major revision, with re-review.

OmniProt integrates MaxQuant, FragPipe, and DIA-NN outputs through a single
downstream workflow (QC, normalization, imputation, differential expression,
enrichment) in one accessible web environment, which is a genuinely useful goal.
However, the submission does not support its transparency and reproducibility
claims: the "open-source OmniProtR package" is a folder of scripts that does not
install, has no tests, and contains hard-coded developer paths, and with no
example data and no figure-regeneration recipe, none of the manuscript's
analyses can be reproduced. Compounding this, the core methods (VSN, KNN,
Bayesian PCA, the enrichment test, and the DEA model) are re-implemented
in-house, cited only to the platform, and not validated against the established
reference tools, so their correctness is currently unverifiable; separately, the
enrichment test uses the whole gene-set library rather than the quantified
proteome as background (which inflates significance and is undocumented), and the
main biological validation rests on an unavailable in-house dataset, making it
circular. These issues are correctable but go to the heart of the paper's
claims, which is why I recommend major revision with re-review rather than a
lighter decision.

Confidential note on potential conflict: I develop differential-expression
software in this area, so I have deliberately framed the attribution point around
the general class of established proteomics DEA frameworks rather than any single
package, including my own. Please weigh my comments with that in mind.
