Recommendation: Major revision with re-review.

OmniProt targets a genuine need -- a single environment that carries MaxQuant, FragPipe,
and DIA-NN outputs through QC, normalization, imputation, differential expression, and
enrichment -- and integration of this kind is a legitimate contribution. However, the
paper's central claims are not currently supported. The "open-source OmniProtR package
... for full transparency and reproducibility" is in practice a folder of R scripts with
no DESCRIPTION/NAMESPACE that fails to install (R CMD INSTALL), contains hard-coded
absolute paths, and ships no tests or example data, so no result can be reproduced by an
independent reader. The manuscript also uses established methods (VSN, quantile
normalization, KNN and Bayesian PCA imputation, the moderated t-test, and GO/KEGG
enrichment) without primary citations or any validation, and the methods are
underspecified (test and background details, FDR, imputation and normalization
parameters), so correctness cannot be assessed. These are reproducibility- and
correctness-level issues that require re-evaluation after revision rather than editorial
polish; I therefore recommend major revision with re-review.

Note: I have a potential conflict of interest, as I am an author of one of the
established differential-expression frameworks (prolfqua) relevant to this manuscript. I
have framed the attribution point around the general class of established tools rather
than any single package.
